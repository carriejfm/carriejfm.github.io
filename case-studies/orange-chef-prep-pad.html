Prep Pad: designed for millennial wellness, loved by bodybuilders and the diabetes community

Orange Chef // Design founder & Director // 2013–2015
Opportunity

Nutrition tracking at the time was tedious, inaccurate, and in conflict with the habits of health-conscious folks assembling meals at home. Eating better was blocked by manual, after-the-fact nutrition calculation. Nest had just been acquired by Google. IoT and the "smart kitchen" were suddenly hot. Orange Chef was a small team betting on connected hardware for nutrition — and I joined the team as founding designer.

The goal: make nutrition tracking disappear into the act of cooking, using a bluetooth scale and real-time weight-based, ingredient-level calculation.

Listening to the audience we got, not the one we planned for

Qualitative research with nutritionists and health-minded millennials gave us personas, needs, and jobs-to-be-done early on. From there we kept our ear to the ground with UX testing, user feedback forums, and using the product ourselves.

The diabetes and bodybuilding communities were audiences we hadn't planned on, but they quickly became core consumer segments with zero marketing dollars. Bodybuilders were dedicated to tracking in a way that kept motivation high, meal after meal. The most meaningful organic audience, though, was people living with diabetes — they often relied on packaged foods because cooking at home with raw ingredients brought ambiguity and risk that labels didn't. With Prep Pad, that changed. To design for this community with real empathy, I tracked my own blood sugars for a week.

"Being able to cook at home for my child, with real ingredients — not just something packaged with a label — has changed everything."

— Parent of child living with diabetes

Low-friction meal-making

Bringing technology into the kitchen, where attention is already scattered was a challenge. We needed fast, glanceable interfaces with search-and-select patterns that could be done single-handed.

When I realized the friction that typing and selecting ingredients created, I designed quick-ingredient favorites that appeared on screen when a weight was detected on Prep Pad, eliminating the need to tap and type in a search. I introduced search result load-in states to avoid moving targets and chunkier tap-targets to reduce selection error. We integrated with the USDA database for quick label scan and ingredient lookup, and designed custom ingredient nutrition entry for anything the database missed. Auto-taring was built into each ingredient as they stacked, so that building the meal was tracking the meal. Leftovers were easiest of all — a single tap on yesterday's meal showed the nutrition breakdown based on the re-plate weight.

Real-time visualization

Based on nutritionist advice we set a standard ideal for macros — protein, carbs and fat — and gave users real-time feedback on how close they were to meeting it. We tested multiple data visualizations with our audience and landed on a dynamic pie chart.

An outer ring showed users their macro goal, and an inner ring shifted with every gram on the Prep Pad. The alignment to the macro goal was summarized in a single glanceable 'balance score' in the middle, with total calories displayed inline with the ingredient list. A full rundown on meal micronutrients was just a swipe away. Later, we allowed users to set their own macro balance goals to ensure the score matched their personal nutrition needs.

Platforms and partnerships

With iPad as the primary cooking surface — larger, easy to view and tap from the counter — we also built out iPhone for on-the-go tracking and review, and an Apple Watch experience with glanceable macro targets and workout-informed nudges.

I fostered design partnerships and designed app experiences with Apple Health and Jawbone for smart nutrition recommendations based on sleep and workout activity, and co-designed a 'smart' nutrition jug with Vitamix that auto-tared on Prep Pad and pulled up recipe recommendations. In market we partnered with Williams-Sonoma stores with bespoke in-store product merchandising displays.

Everything else

We were a small team and I threw myself into everything product design adjacent — designing the website, directing photo shoots, scripting and directing our launch video, and overseeing packaging design. I temporarily stepped into the product manager role to keep the ship steady just before acquisition.

"Her knack in leading a team of designers and fine tuning interfaces makes her invaluable. In addition, she took up product management responsibilities and superseded all our expectations."

AI in Practice

Then: We explored NLP on packaging so users could snap pictures instead of scanning finicky labels or typing in ingredients — the limit of that era still meant initiating, aiming and confirming ingredients. We were also exploring a voice-control Alexa app for hands-free navigation of meal building.

Now: Today I would push for an integrated agent that could look at the raw food itself via a snap or voice description — identifying ingredients, estimating portions, and logging automatically. No typing, no label hunting. Combined with the bluetooth scale, that would truly allow tracking to disappear into the meal-making task. Same philosophy as the original Prep Pad, just executed with tools we didn't have in 2013.

Reflection

Being a founding designer at a small startup is woven into my DNA. It stretched me in ways no other role has, clarifying both my growing edges and what I actually want from my work. "Doing it all" can feel energising, and sometimes necessary, but protecting focus on my own discipline matters more. Saying no, so I could do the most important work well, wasn't a limitation — it was essential to the health of the product, the team, and myself.

Managing a discipline isn't the same as practising one, either. I stepped into people management before I was truly ready, and the footing I found came with sharp lessons: the value of direct feedback, the importance of role and individual fit, and that creating the right environment for someone to thrive matters more than being liked.

If I could revisit one decision, I would push harder to reposition Prep Pad around the organic audience we actually found, rather than holding onto a broader wellness identity. It reinforced something I now hold closely: the product you ship and the product people love can be different, and owning that gap is where the real work begins.
