Context

REBUILD OF AFTERPAY DESIGN AFTERPAY

Opportunity

Afterpay operates in the Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) space, where consumer trust and regulation are critical to growth.

In 2024, the business was in the middle of global BNPL regulatory shift, requiring rapid coordination across regions and disciplines while juggling a rebrand within the US region and continuing to drive growth.

At the same time, the system was under strain. Design was under-resourced, brought in late, and often asked to execute predefined solutions. Roadmap priorities were volatile, and teams were beginning to ship without design input.

REBUILD OF AFTERPAY DESIGN AFTERPAY

Goal

Rebuild the design discipline, and shift from execution to strategic partnership — while fulfilling urgent regulatory deadlines.

REBUILD OF AFTERPAY DESIGN AFTERPAY

Role: Head of design

Responsible - Lead and managed product design team, individual contributor on key experiences

Accountable - Contributor to roadmap priorities with strategic design input and resourcing

Consulted - Member of the regional Australia and New Zealand leadership chapter

Approach

REBUILD OF AFTERPAY DESIGN AFTERPAY

Hypothesis

I believe teams that understand the problem, ship better solutions than teams that execute predefined ones

REBUILD OF AFTERPAY DESIGN AFTERPAY

Rebuild team and trust

I rebuilt the team from the ground up, establishing clear ownership across work streams and introducing best practices in team rituals and tooling.

A culture of clear feedback and psychological safety was grown with clarity in roles, consistency in the day-to-day and reconnection with the broader design org. I stepped into difficult conversations with adjacent teams to mediate and reset.

I coached individuals through challenging situations, including unblocking a high-performing, autonomous designer from a performance misunderstanding.

Solution-led to problem-led

I shifted the team from executing predefined solutions to defining problems. Through the unpacking of regulatory, business, and behavioral drivers we aligned as a team to redefine and understand opportunities.

Designers worked with measurable success criteria in mind and solved problems with true ownership. It made for better cross-functional culture and better delivery outcomes.

REBUILD OF AFTERPAY DESIGN AFTERPAY

Cross-functional alignment

I established consistent alignment across product, engineering, legal, and compliance through a shared roadmap with design capacity visibility, regular 1:1s with product partners and actively embedding research and JTBDs into roadmap and PRDs.

When last-minute requests came up or scope thrashed (as they do), we had the footing to handle it — allowing us to reprioritize, make better tradeoffs or push back if needed. Embedded within the regional leadership team, I influenced strategy and presented to C-suite.

A regulated Afterpay →

Aligning Afterpay to responsible buy now pay later lending obligations included the design and redesign of key experiences and required variation from region to region:

Credit limit increases, onboarding improvements, user-initiated spend controls to support responsible usage, KYC and compliance flows, autopay to reduce late fees, fraud protection and dispute flows.

I had 2 designers focused on regulation workstreams.

I co-wrote a reusable regulation playbook with product to enable consistent execution across US, AU, NZ and kickstart proactive conversations with UK regulatory bodies.

Reflection

Helping the Afterpay product team get its mojo back was enormously rewarding, and hard-won. It proved what I already knew: the health of the systems and teams we work in drives product outcomes.

My hypothesis was true — the design team shipped solutions that objectively improved the Afterpay product and business.

It's also true that change only comes with trust, and that path isn't always straightforward. Proving new ways of working took time, consistency, and repeated clarity. Not everyone loved the changes from the beginning, and I didn't always nail the approach.

I owned my missteps quickly, asked for feedback, and tried again. I can say with a lot of pride and confidence that I stepped out of this role a year later with a cross-functional team that truly trusted and valued design partnership.
