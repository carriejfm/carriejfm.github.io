Context

MONEY STORIES BLOCK

Opportunity

The 2021 Afterpay was scaling and had embarked on a new proposition: Money by Afterpay — a money-positive neobank-style product for the existing Afterpay consumer base. A year into the covid digital surge, the Afterpay audience wasn't asking for a spreadsheet or dashboard to help manage their finances; they were living on Instagram, consuming Reels, swiping stories.

I was brought in as a principal to help Millennials and Gen Z 'feel' their money. Banking UX really had no native format for the behavior young money-anxious users were already fluent in. I put two and two together and Money Stories was born.

Goal: make money feel as flick-through and actionable as Reels — turning raw spending data into small, personalized moments of insight and low-barrier-to-entry action.

MONEY STORIES BLOCK

Making finances accessible

My hunch was that a low-effort digital content pattern the audience was already familiar with — in a low-stress environment — would lower the barrier to entry and help people change their feelings and behavior within the high-stress money environment. Like Nike Training Club made fitness feel bite-sized and manageable, Money Stories would make money feel simple and accessible.

The core concept was articulated as a flywheel: Data → Content → Action, repeating daily and weekly. Money insight stories were generated from the user's own data and delivered as bite-size content, ending in a one-tap action.

I'd been in the role weeks when I pushed back on leadership to keep Money Stories in the founder deck. We had the qual backing, I had the conviction — and I was right.

MONEY STORIES BLOCK

Designing the format

I designed a reel of avatars at eye level on the home surface, just below the hero balance. Three data-based story types on three distinct cadences: Daily Spend, Afterpay Spend, and Weekly Insights — each ending in a one-tap action.

Weekly Insights dropped every Monday with bite-sized data on where last week's money went across Wants, Needs and savings. Each drop ended with an Outro — a deliberately mindful bookend that helped users pause and ground in positivity.

MONEY STORIES BLOCK

A franchise, not a feature

I worked with my marketing partner to harness media partnerships that would make Money Stories about more than just self-reflection.

Pedestrian Makes Cents — a co-created editorial franchise that polled communities on Instagram on Tuesday and dropped the visualised results in-app the following Tuesday, linking to a long-form Pedestrian article. This story format held a 55% engagement rate.

Refinery29 Money Diaries in-app weekly, Calm Money Mindfulness co-design, and a Lil Nas X talent series in flight — three editorial partnerships that made Money Stories a content franchise.
